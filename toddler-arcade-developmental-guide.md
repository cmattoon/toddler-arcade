# Toddler Arcade: Developmental Activity & Progression Guide

**Status:** Design draft v0.1 · **Audience:** Game contributors, parents, educators, and therapists reviewing the design

## Purpose

Toddler Arcade is a small-button/joystick play platform for young children, including children with developmental differences. A child should be able to enjoy the same game world through several years of changing interests and abilities—from deliberately making something happen to matching, sorting, sequencing, remembering, and solving problems.

**Design promise:** Every mechanic has an intelligible reason to exist. Discovery and enjoyment are valid reasons; noise and flashing lights are not substitutes for interaction or learning.

This document is a **design taxonomy, not a developmental assessment**, therapy plan, diagnostic tool, age-equivalence chart, or claim that a game improves clinical outcomes. It draws general concepts from DRDP, Head Start's Early Learning Outcomes Framework (ELOF), ASHA communication milestones, and play/executive-function guidance. The taxonomy and example sequences below are original design proposals, **not official items or prescribed milestone orders**.

## Operating assumptions

- The child can press large buttons but may not read, type, speak, or reliably use a joystick.
- Every essential activity works with buttons. Joystick-only skills are optional branches.
- A child can demonstrate advanced understanding in one domain while learning basic controls or concepts in another.
- Gameplay should not require speech, rapid responses, a diagnosis, or one preferred sensory style.
- Help from an adult is welcome; game results must never be interpreted as proof of independent developmental mastery.
- Keep earlier, exploratory activities available as more complex ones are introduced.

## Three independent axes

| Axis                      | What it changes                            | Example                                                                             |
|---------------------------|--------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Conceptual complexity** | The kind of knowledge or thinking required | Match identical apples → sort fruit vs vegetables → gather ingredients for a recipe |
| **Task difficulty**       | The demand *within* that concept           | 2 choices → 6 choices; clear examples → similar distractors                         |
| **Support**               | How an adult/game scaffolds the activity   | Model the action → show a hint → let the child act independently                    |

This guide defines **conceptual complexity** only. Do not implement a single child-wide Level 1–10 or mistake these examples for universal developmental steps. A game world can have several independent activity branches.

## Skill catalog (24 tags)

The arrows describe **possible advances in conceptual demand**, not a fixed clinical sequence. Each skill can be practiced through multiple game worlds.

### A. Exploration and reasoning

| Tag    | Skill                          | Possible progression                                                                              | Arcade activity ideas                                        |
|--------|--------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| **A1** | Intentional cause and effect   | Discover an effect → repeat it deliberately → choose between distinct effects                     | Bubble Machine, Button Orchestra, Train Whistle, Light Lab   |
| **A2** | Prediction and experimentation | Anticipate a familiar outcome → compare two outcomes → change a variable intentionally            | Ball Ramps, Weather Machine, Color Mixer, Water Playground   |
| **A3** | Goal-directed problem-solving  | Try an action → reuse a successful strategy → combine actions → try another strategy when blocked | Crane Rescue, Fix the Robot, Bridge Builder, Rescue the Duck |

### B. Visual thinking and classification

| Tag    | Skill               | Possible progression                                                                                    | Arcade activity ideas                                        |
|--------|---------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| **B1** | Identical matching  | Match identical images → match different views/depictions of the same familiar item                     | Lost Socks, Animal Twins, Parcel Match, Picture Lotto        |
| **B2** | Attribute matching  | Match by color **or** shape → ignore an irrelevant attribute → match color **and** shape                | Shape Garden, Balloon Shop, Cookie Cutters, Alien Designer   |
| **B3** | Classification      | Group identical things → group by broad category → classify by function → reclassify under another rule | Grocery Sorter, Farm Pens, Recycling Center, Toy Shelves     |
| **B4** | Patterns            | Recognize repeated elements → copy a pattern → extend a pattern → construct a pattern                   | Bead String, Train Cars, Caterpillar Colors, Light Parade    |
| **B5** | Part–whole thinking | Complete a missing piece → assemble an object from parts → select parts for a functional whole          | Face Builder, Vehicle Workshop, Simple Jigsaw, Build a House |

### C. Spatial understanding

| Tag    | Skill                    | Possible progression                                                                                            | Arcade activity ideas                                        |
|--------|--------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| **C1** | Directional control      | Choose a side → move toward a visible target → coordinate multiple directional moves                            | Train, Crane, Elevator, Space Rover                          |
| **C2** | Positional relationships | In/out → on/under/beside → relate multiple objects in a scene                                                   | Teddy's House, Garage Parking, Hide the Mouse, Picnic Setup  |
| **C3** | Navigation and planning  | Go directly to a destination → follow a route → detour around an obstacle → visit targets in a deliberate order | Mail Delivery, Railway Switchyard, Maze Explorer, River Boat |

### D. Language, concepts, and representation

| Tag    | Skill                          | Possible progression                                                                                             | Arcade activity ideas                                   |
|--------|--------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|
| **D1** | Word–object/action association | Recognize familiar picture/sound → choose item named aloud → distinguish action and descriptive words            | Animal Finder, Sound Safari, Find My Toy, Grocery Store |
| **D2** | Understanding directions       | Respond to a familiar single request → select object + action → execute two related steps → interpret qualifiers | Robot Helper, Car Wash, Make a Sandwich, Pet Care       |
| **D3** | Symbolic/pretend play          | Activate a familiar pretend action → combine everyday actions → enact a purposeful multi-step scenario           | Teddy Café, Animal Hospital, Fire Station, Play Kitchen |

*Notes:* Spoken instructions are an optional presentation mode, not a prerequisite. Audio and pictures can be used together. An arcade choice may support communication but does not, by itself, demonstrate speech-language acquisition.

### E. Memory, attention, and flexible rules

| Tag    | Skill                  | Possible progression                                                                                       | Arcade activity ideas                                                    |
|--------|------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **E1** | Location/object memory | Find an object just hidden → remember its location after a delay → track a moved hiding place              | Peekaboo Doors, Shell Game, Find the Egg, Hidden Presents                |
| **E2** | Sequence memory        | Repeat one demonstrated action → reproduce a short ordered sequence → use a remembered sequence for a goal | Simon Lights, Drum Echo, Robot Dance, Footstep Trail                     |
| **E3** | Start/stop and waiting | Start/stop in response to a cue → wait for a signal → choose when a response is appropriate                | Red Light/Green Light, Stop the Train, Sleeping Dragon, Traffic Crossing |
| **E4** | Changing rules         | Follow a consistent rule → switch to a new rule → choose a rule from a visible context cue                 | Sorting Switch, Color Rules, Robot Commands, Traffic Controller          |

### F. Early mathematical thinking

| Tag    | Skill                     | Possible progression                                                                                     | Arcade activity ideas                                     |
|--------|---------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|
| **F1** | One-to-one correspondence | One press creates one item → give one item to each recipient → account for unmatched items               | Block Tower, Feed the Animals, Egg Cartons, Mailboxes     |
| **F2** | Quantity comparison       | Notice one vs many → choose more/less → identify equal quantities → compare differently arranged groups  | Cookie Bowls, Aquarium, Picnic Plates, Block Scales       |
| **F3** | Counting and symbols      | Count objects/actions with support → relate a quantity to a number word → associate numeral and quantity | Counting Farm, Dice Builder, Parking Garage, Number Train |

### G. Social participation and agency

| Tag    | Skill                        | Possible progression                                                                             | Arcade activity ideas                                      |
|--------|------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------|
| **G1** | Meaningful choice            | Select a preferred outcome → ask for a repeat/change → choose between alternative goals          | Jukebox, Toy Vending Machine, Movie Picker, More Bubbles   |
| **G2** | Imitation                    | Copy a demonstrated single action → copy two actions → invent an action for someone else to copy | Copycat, Dance Robot, Drum Echo, Mirror Me                 |
| **G3** | Turn-taking and shared goals | Alternate simple actions with a partner → initiate an exchange → cooperate on one outcome        | Duet Drums, Passing Train, Build Together, Two-Driver Boat |

These social tags only meaningfully apply when another person participates. A screen character pretending to take turns is not evidence of social reciprocity with a person.

### Worked example: Grocery Store

These are **activity stages**, not a validated age progression.

| Stage             | Child's task                                                | What's conceptually new?                                      |
|-------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Explore**       | Press apple and watch an apple move into a basket           | Intentional action and consequence                            |
| **Same Item**     | Apples into apple basket; oranges into orange basket        | Match identity                                                |
| **Same Category** | Apples and oranges into fruit; carrots into vegetables      | Abstract a broader category beyond identical pictures         |
| **Find It**       | Choose the item named or described                          | Map a label/attribute to an item; independent language branch |
| **Shopping List** | Gather a specified set of different items                   | Maintain a multi-item goal; may add memory demand             |
| **Recipe**        | Gather ingredients and apply them in an order               | Multi-step planning and symbolic routine                      |
| **Substitutions** | Choose a suitable replacement when something is unavailable | Functional relationships and flexible problem-solving         |

**Crucial:** The child need not finish Find It before learning Recipe, or recognize numerals to gather ingredients. These branches can coexist. Avoid making unnecessary skills into gates.

## Game contributor specification

Every contributed activity should answer these questions in plain English:

```yaml
world: grocery-store
activity: category-sort
primary_tags: [B3]
secondary_tags: [B2]
concept: "Different items can belong to the same broader group."
child_action: "Select a basket for each item using large buttons."
complexity_step: "Sort fruit vs vegetables instead of apples vs oranges."
not_complexity: "Do not call more baskets or a timer a new concept."
feedback: "Show where the item went and the other members of that group."
possible_next_activities: [find-it, shopping-list, sorting-switch]
real_world_extension: "Sort familiar groceries with an adult."
```

### Review questions for every activity

1. **What is the action?** Describe it without naming buttons or software events.
2. **What concept is exercised?** Identify one **primary** tag, plus optional secondary tags.
3. **What is the next conceptual step?** Name the new relationship, rule, or reasoning demand.
4. **What is merely harder?** Keep speed, choices, number of distractors, and time limits outside the concept progression.
5. **What does feedback explain?** Prefer an intelligible consequence to arbitrary celebration; celebrate meaningfully and don't punish exploratory mistakes.
6. **Can it be played with buttons?** Avoid required reading, speech, fine motor precision, or accurate joystick use.
7. **Could an adult do it with real objects?** Suggest a related offscreen activity where one is natural.
8. **Is there a limitation?** Note when the software cannot distinguish understanding from guessing, memorized input sequences, or motor difficulty.

## Tagging and presentation

- **Parent-facing filters:** Matching & Sorting, Patterns, Memory, Cause & Effect, Spatial Skills, Language & Concepts, Early Math, Attention & Rules, Social Play.
- **Developer-facing tags:** Stable IDs A1–G3 from the catalog; 1 primary tag per activity; secondary tags only when the mechanic genuinely exercises them.
- **Complexity:** Named variants such as `same-item`, `same-category`, `recipe`; don't use a cross-game maturity score or absolute 'developmental age.'
- **Difficulty:** Later configurable independently (choices, distractors, prompts, visual similarity, etc.).
- **Progress:** No clinical scores or therapy-goal tracking required. Parent selects activities; software can remember optional UI settings without profiling the child.

## Boundaries and quality bar

The goal is to provide **purposeful, reusable play opportunities**, not digital therapy. Similar-looking behaviors can have different explanations. A child may understand an instruction but not operate the joystick, or may win by trial and error without understanding the classification rule. Success in a game does not establish mastery in real life, and a source framework's validity does not automatically validate a game built around it.

Avoid autism stereotypes or a universal 'autism mode.' Offer ordinary, respectful defaults: predictable controls, understandable feedback, and no compulsory sensory effects. Content and preferred themes should be independent of developmental tags.

Prefer a small set of coherent game worlds with multiple developmental activity branches over dozens of unrelated noisy mini-games. Keep free play available and enjoyable throughout.

## Research foundations (conceptual alignment, not endorsements or validation)

1. **Desired Results Developmental Profile (DRDP 2025), Infant/Toddler Measures at a Glance:** https://www.draccess.org/DRDP2025MeasuresAtaGlanceIT.html — Source categories include ATL 3 Problem-Solving, COG 1 Imitation, MATH 1 Spatial Thinking, MATH 2 Classification, MATH 3 Number and Counting, SCI 1 Cause and Effect, and FLD 1 Understanding Language.
2. **DRDP 2025, Preschool–TK–Kindergarten Measures at a Glance:** https://draccess.org/DRDP2025MeasuresAtaGlancePTK.html — Extends the view into planning, number operations, measurement, and other preschool areas.
3. **Head Start Early Learning Outcomes Framework:** https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-HE23_1100-PURL-gpo65456/pdf/GOVPUB-HE23_1100-PURL-gpo65456.pdf — Broad developmental domains and goal progressions; an educational framework, not this app's scoring scheme.
4. **ASHA communication milestones:** https://www.asha.org/public/developmental-milestones/communication-milestones/ — Reference for language comprehension and communication opportunities; not a game assessment.
5. **Harvard Center on the Developing Child, Brain-Building Through Play:** https://developingchild.harvard.edu/resources/handouts-tools/brainbuildingthroughplay/ — Playful, adult-supported activities involving attention, memory, and self-control.
6. **CDC developmental milestone checklist explanation:** https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/key-points.html — CDC explicitly says these checklists are not developmental standards or diagnostic/screening tools; use only as broad context.
